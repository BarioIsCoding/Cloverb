\paragraph{Recipe.} The final model has 9.22M parameters: $d=256$, 10 post-norm blocks with 8 heads,
ReLU$^2$ MLPs of width $3d$, geometry-initialised static attention biases, no dynamic templates, and the
captured piece in the move history. Six pools of 2M positions each, drawn with the training sampler,
were labelled once by Maia-3-23M-ponder (top-16 moves; about 85 minutes per pool at 390
positions/s). Training uses AdamW ($\beta=(0.9, 0.98)$, weight decay 0.05, learning rate $10^{-3}$,
1k warm-up steps), batch 512, gradient clipping at 1, an EMA of the weights with decay 0.9998, and
bf16 autocast, at about 280 positions/s.

\paragraph{Switching to pure distillation mid-run.} For the first 24k steps the target mixed the
teacher's distribution (weight 0.7) with the human move (0.3); below 30\,s on the mover's clock,
where the teacher never trained, the mix was 0.4/0.6. Projecting the learning curve showed this
would land near Maia-3-5M, so at step 24k we branched two 3k-step learning-rate cool-downs from
the same checkpoint on identical batches. With the human move removed outside time trouble, the
cooled-down model reached a cross-entropy of 1.3970 and 54.36\% top-1, against 1.3996 and 54.19\%
with the mixed target. Its raw (non-EMA) cross-entropy was already 0.008 lower halfway through the
cool-down, consistent with less gradient noise. The run continued from step 24k with pure teacher
targets (still 0.4/0.6 under 30\,s) to 80k steps. By step 48k the constant-rate phase gained only
0.001 nats per 2k steps, so the linear decay to 2\% of the peak rate was started there, covering the
last 40\% of training instead of the planned 30\%. In total the model sees about 41M samples, 3.4 passes over the pool.

\paragraph{How far distillation can go.} A student trained to match a teacher approaches the
teacher's accuracy from below. Table~\ref{tab:teachers} scores every Maia-3 checkpoint and their
averages on the 10k development subset of the ALLIE test sample. The clock-aware teacher we use is 0.2 points
below Maia-3-79M; the best ensemble (79M, 23M and 23M-ponder, averaged logits) is 0.3 points above
it. Distillation alone can therefore at most edge past the strongest Maia-3 under Maia's protocol.
Beating it clearly needs what a teacher cannot supply: far more human moves, or information the
teacher does not see. Cloverb's clock, increment and 64-ply history are such information, and they
matter most in exactly the positions Maia's protocol removes.
